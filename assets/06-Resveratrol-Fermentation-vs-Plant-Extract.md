Resveratrol (trans-3,5,4'-trihydroxystilbene) is one of the most studied bioactive molecules in modern cosmetics and nutrition. ## Purity Profile: Fermentation vs. Plant Extraction

### The Plant Extraction Reality

Resveratrol extracted from *Polygonum cuspidatum* typically ranges from **50% to 98% purity** depending on extraction and purification methods. The 50% grade is common in bulk markets but carries substantial impurities, including:

- **Emodin**: A naturally occurring anthraquinone in knotweed that can cause gastrointestinal irritation and skin sensitization. Regulators in several markets have flagged emodin as a concern in topical and oral products.
- **Other stilbenes**: Polydatin (piceid), resveratroloside, and other related compounds that vary by harvest season and growing region.
- **Pesticide residues**: Knotweed is often cultivated in regions with limited agricultural oversight, and residual pesticides (including organophosphates and pyrethroids) have been detected in commercial extracts.
- **Heavy metals**: Soil contamination — particularly lead, arsenic, and cadmium — can accumulate in root-based extracts.

Even "98% pure" plant-extracted resveratrol may contain trace emodin (typically 0.1–0.5%) and residual solvents from the extraction process.

## Consistency: Why Batch Variability Matters

### The Problem with Botanical Sources

Plant-derived resveratrol content varies dramatically based on:

- **Growing region**: Soil composition, altitude, and climate all affect biosynthesis
- **Harvest season**: Resveratrol concentration peaks at specific growth stages
- **Plant age and part**: Root vs. stem vs. leaf extraction yields different profiles
- **Processing lag**: Degradation begins immediately after harvest

In practice, this means a brand using knotweed-derived resveratrol may see **±15% variation in active content** across batches. For formulators, this creates several downstream problems:

1. **Reformulation risk**: Color, odor, and efficacy can shift between batches
2. **Stability inconsistencies**: Impurity profiles affect shelf-life predictions
3. **Regulatory documentation**: Each lot requires full re-testing to confirm compliance
4. **Consumer experience**: Product feel and performance can change seasonally

## Supply Chain Resilience

### Vulnerabilities in Plant-Based Supply

The global resveratrol supply chain from botanical sources faces structural risks:

- **Geographic concentration**: Over 85% of *Polygonum cuspidatum* extraction capacity is concentrated in a single region, creating single-source vulnerability
- **Climate impact**: Droughts, floods, and temperature extremes directly affect harvest yields
- **Agricultural labor volatility**: Harvest-dependent supply chains are sensitive to labor availability and wage inflation
- **Long lead times**: 6–12 months from harvest to finished extract
- **Minimum order constraints**: Seasonal harvest cycles create boom-bust pricing patterns

### Fermentation Supply: Predictable, Scalable, Localizable

Fermentation-based production decouples resveratrol supply from agricultural cycles. Alvokor's manufacturing model offers:

For brands managing multi-market inventory or just-in-time production schedules, this predictability directly reduces working capital requirements and stockout risk.
