### Erythritol as a Fossil-Free Chemical Raw Material for ESG Supply Chains

Erythritol is produced through **glucose fermentation** using non-GMO yeast strains. The production pathway is:

**Renewable glucose feedstock → Fermentation → Crystallization → Erythritol**

This process is fundamentally **fossil-free**:

- **Feedstock**: Glucose derived from corn starch (renewable, annually replanted)
- **Process energy**: Fermentation operates at ambient temperatures (vs. petrochemical synthesis at 200–400°C)
- **No petrochemical intermediates**: The entire molecular structure is assembled by biological catalysis
- **Byproduct valorization**: Fermentation byproducts can be used as animal feed or biogas feedstock

**ESG reporting advantage:** Erythritol sourced from fermentation processes enables quantifiable Scope 3 emissions reductions in ESG reports — a direct, verifiable metric that petrochemical sweeteners cannot provide.

### Citric Acid as a Food Grade Natural Preservative

In sugar-reduced formulations, citric acid is not optional — it's **structurally essential**:

1. **Sweetness enhancement**: Acidity boosts perceived sweetness by 15–20%, reducing the amount of sweetener needed
2. **Taste masking**: Neutralizes off-notes from high-intensity sweeteners (stevia bitterness, monk fruit aftertaste)
3. **Preservation**: Lowers pH to inhibit microbial growth — critical when sugar's preservative function is removed
4. **Chelation**: Sequesters metal ions that catalyze oxidation in reduced-sugar matrices
5. **Color stability**: Maintains anthocyanin and carotenoid stability in functional beverages

**As a fermentation feed additive:** Citric acid is also widely used in animal nutrition as a **feed acidifier**, improving gut health and nutrient absorption in livestock. This dual food/feed positioning makes it a versatile component of the fossil-free supply chain.

### β-Cyclodextrin in Sugar Reduction: Three Critical Functions

1. **Bitterness masking**: Encapsulates hydrophobic bitter compounds in stevia and monk fruit extracts, reducing their interaction with bitter taste receptors while preserving sweetness
2. **Flavor encapsulation**: Protects volatile flavor compounds that become more perceptible in reduced-sugar products (where sugar no longer masks off-flavors)
3. **Stability enhancement**: Shields sensitive actives from oxidation, light, and heat during processing and shelf life

## FAQ

### Q1: Is erythritol truly fossil-free?
**A:** Yes. Erythritol is produced by fermenting glucose (derived from corn starch) using yeast. No petrochemical feedstocks, intermediates, or catalysts are involved in the production process. This is verifiable through supply chain documentation.

### Q2: How does erythritol compare to xylitol in terms of ESG profile?
**A:** Both are fermentation-derived polyols. However, erythritol has a lower caloric value (0–0.2 vs. 2.4 kcal/g), higher digestive tolerance, and zero glycemic impact — making it more suitable for diabetic-friendly and calorie-reduction formulations. Both are fossil-free.

### Q4: Is your citric acid suitable for pharmaceutical applications?

### Q5: How does beta-cyclodextrin improve stevia taste?
**A:** β-CD encapsulates the hydrophobic bitter-tasting compounds in stevia extracts (primarily stevioside and Reb C), reducing their interaction with bitter taste receptors while leaving the sweet Reb A component free to activate sweet receptors. The result is a cleaner, more sugar-like taste profile.
